# FPGA power and configuration architecture — design in progress

## Supply order

The iCE40UP5K-SG48I uses three distinct FPGA supply levels in this design. The proposed startup order is:

1. An external regulated 5 V input passes through a resettable fuse and series Schottky diode to `P5V`. USB VBUS is a sense signal only and is not connected to the supply input. `P5V` feeds an always-enabled 1.2 V regulator for `VCC` and, through the recommended RC filter, `VCCPLL`.
2. A core-rail power-good output enables the 3.3 V regulator for `SPI_VCCIO1`, `VCCIO0`, `VCCIO2`, configuration flash, oscillator, and all FT2232H 3.3 V supplies.
3. A 3.3 V power-good output enables the 2.5 V regulator for `VPP_2V5`.
4. `CRESET_B` remains low until all FPGA rails reach their minimum operating voltages, then rises to initiate master-SPI configuration. The programming interface must also be able to assert it low while writing the flash.

The [Lattice UltraPlus data sheet](https://www.latticesemi.com/view_document?document_id=51968), sections 4.2–4.6, specifies 1.14–1.26 V for `VCC` and `VCCPLL`, 2.30–3.46 V for `VPP_2V5` in master-SPI mode, and 1.20–3.46 V for the I/O supplies. The chosen 3.3 V I/O standard must satisfy its tighter I/O-specific limits. Lattice recommends `VCC`/`VCCPLL` first, `SPI_VCCIO1` after they have reached at least 0.5 V, and `VPP_2V5` after `SPI_VCCIO1` has reached at least 0.5 V. The other I/O rails may rise after the core. The [captured schematic](../hardware/power_config.tsx) implements this power-good cascade, but physical ramp compliance and reset timing have not been measured.

The data sheet requires monotonic supply ramps of 0.6–10 V/ms. Regulator enable ordering by itself does not prove compliance: output capacitance, startup current, soft-start behavior, and load must be checked for each selected part. On a physical board, the three ramps and `CRESET_B` must be checked with an oscilloscope. The FPGA's internal POR monitors only `VCC`, `SPI_VCCIO1`, and `VPP_2V5`; the other I/O rails still must be present for normal operation. An external reset circuit must hold or pulse `CRESET_B` low after all rails meet their operating minima.

## Decoupling and interfaces

The [Lattice hardware checklist](https://www.latticesemi.com/view_document?document_id=47779), section 2.1, recommends 4.7 µF and 100 nF at each `VCC`, `VCCIO`, `SPI_VCCIO1`, and `VPP_2V5` supply pin, and a 100 Ω feed with 4.7 µF and 100 nF for `VCCPLL`. These are the starting values for the schematic. The [SG48 pinout workbook, revision 1.1](https://www.latticesemi.com/view_document?document_id=51971) lists `VCC` at pins 5 and 30, `VCCPLL` at 29, `VCCIO_0` at 33, `SPI_VCCIO1` at 22, `VCCIO_2` at 1, and `VPP_2V5` at 24. It lists ground on the exposed paddle and no separate `GNDPLL` package pin. The proposed decoupling must cover each of those supply pins; placement and return-path quality remain to be reviewed.

The USB bridge, oscillator, flash, and host-facing outputs must not drive unpowered FPGA pins. Powering their 3.3 V domain only after the core is one part of that control. Configuration-bus ownership during flash programming still needs a complete electrical and host-sequence review. The proposed four-layer board will keep the ground return continuous under the clock, flash, and USB paths and place local capacitors close to their pins.

## Regulator candidate review

TI's [LP5912 data sheet](https://www.ti.com/document-viewer/LP5912/datasheet) and orderable-part pages list 1.2 V, 3.3 V and 2.5 V versions of the 500 mA WSON regulator. Its specified output tolerance is ±3% below 3.3 V and ±2% at 3.3 V, so the nominal steady-state ranges would be 1.164–1.236 V, 3.234–3.366 V and 2.425–2.575 V. These fit the broad FPGA supply ranges, assuming board noise and transient excursions remain inside the remaining margin. At the initial rail budgets, the idealized LDO dissipation from 5 V would be approximately 0.57 W, 0.34 W and 0.075 W respectively. TI gives a 71.2 °C/W junction-to-ambient figure on a specified high-conductivity test board with two thermal vias; that is not a prediction for this PCB.

This family is **rejected for the current rail topology**. TI specifies a 10 µF maximum effective output capacitance, while Lattice's suggested per-pin decoupling already totals 9.6 µF nominal on the two-pin 1.2 V core rail and 14.4 µF nominal on the three-pin 3.3 V FPGA I/O rail, before the regulator capacitor and other 3.3 V loads are added. Capacitor tolerance and DC-bias loss do not provide a sound basis for asserting stability. The data sheet also gives a typical 200 µs turn-on time to 95% output without a guaranteed ramp-rate limit. A 3.3 V output reaching 95% in that typical time corresponds to about 15.7 V/ms on average, above the FPGA's 10 V/ms maximum if the rise were approximately linear. Its actual ramp may differ, but the available figures do not prove compliance. The power-good output has an undefined state below 1.6 V input, so a simple power-good pull-up to VBUS needs a startup-state analysis before it can serve as a guaranteed sequencer. The 1.2 V output cannot directly drive the next LP5912 enable pin because that pin's specified on threshold is 1.3 V. A regulator with adequate capacitive-load range and controlled soft-start is needed.

The adjustable [TPS7A9001DSKR](https://www.ti.com/product/TPS7A90/part-details/TPS7A9001DSKR) is the captured candidate for all three rails: 500 mA maximum output, 0.8–5.7 V adjustable range, power-good, and capacitor-controlled soft-start. Its [data sheet](https://www.ti.com/document-viewer/TPS7A90/datasheet) lists a 10 µF **minimum** effective input and output capacitor rather than LP5912's 10 µF output maximum. Each regulator has a nominal 22 µF capacitor at input and output in the schematic; the selected capacitor part must retain at least 10 µF under DC bias and tolerance. Feedback resistor ratios give nominal outputs of 1.1992 V, 3.328 V, and 2.56 V before resistor and regulator error. With ±1 % resistors and TI's ±1.0 % output accuracy, the static ranges are 1.179–1.219 V, 3.244–3.412 V and 2.499–2.621 V; see [part selection](part-selection.md). The 1.2 V power-good output is pulled to its own rail and enables the 3.3 V regulator; the 3.3 V power-good output enables the 2.5 V regulator. At the earliest specified rising power-good threshold (84% of nominal), these upstream rails exceed Lattice's 0.5 V sequencing threshold before their downstream rails enable. The 1.2 V power-good high level must also exceed the next regulator's 1.1 V enable threshold; at a 1.14 V minimum core rail, a 10 kΩ pull-up with 1 µA maximum leakage loses at most 10 mV, leaving about 30 mV static margin. The specified 0.4 V maximum power-good low voltage equals the next regulator's 0.4 V maximum disabled threshold, so there is no guaranteed low-level noise margin. This needs startup and brownout validation, and a buffer if measurements expose false enabling. The third regulator's power-good signal alone is insufficient to release FPGA reset: 84% of 2.5 V is only 2.10 V, below the 2.30 V minimum for `VPP_2V5` in master-SPI mode.

The three NR/SS capacitors are 6.8 nF nominal. With the data-sheet 4–9 µA charging-current range, a ±10% capacitor assumption, and the ideal soft-start relation `dVOUT/dt = (VOUT/VREF) × I/C`, the calculated nominal-rail slope bounds are approximately 0.80–2.20 V/ms (1.2 V), 2.22–6.12 V/ms (3.3 V), and 1.71–4.71 V/ms (2.5 V). Those bounds sit inside Lattice's 0.6–10 V/ms requirement **only for the idealized capacitor-controlled ramp**. Actual regulator startup, load, rail interaction, capacitor tolerance, and brownout behavior still require circuit-level and then physical verification. Capacitors with worse than ±10% tolerance would invalidate the calculation.

The [TPS389025DSER](https://www.ti.com/product/TPS3890/part-details/TPS389025DSER) is the captured reset-supervisor candidate. Its nominal rising sense threshold is 2.414 V; the family's specified 1% threshold accuracy puts the lower bound at about 2.390 V, above the FPGA's 2.30 V master-SPI minimum. Powered from the already-enabled 3.3 V rail, it monitors `VPP_2V5` and holds `CRESET_B` low through its open-drain reset output until the rail is valid plus a delay. A 10 nF nominal CT capacitor gives approximately 10.7 ms using TI's nominal `t(sec) = C(µF) × 1.07 + 25 µs` equation. `MR` has a 10 kΩ pull-up to 3.3 V and a host-control net for the next integration increment; the eventual host driver must pull it low without driving it high. Supervisor startup and procurement remain to be checked.

For USB, the [FT2232H data sheet](https://www.ftdichip.cn/Support/Documents/DataSheets/ICs/DS_FT2232H.pdf), self-powered Figure 6.3, shows 3.3 V on `VREGIN`, `VPLL`, `VPHY` and all `VCCIO` pins; the bridge's internal regulator output powers its 1.8 V `VCORE` pins with at least 3.3 µF local capacitance. The captured support network also has a 12 MHz crystal, USB data and reference connections, reset, a 93LC46B EEPROM, and VBUS-sensed `PWRSAV#`. Its internal 1.8 V output is for the FTDI subsystem and is not an FPGA supply. Pin and source review is in [usb-ftdi-review.md](usb-ftdi-review.md).

## Remaining design checks

- Orderable capacitors are selected. From Murata's typical DC-bias curve, the 22 µF regulator capacitors retain about 12.7–19.6 µF, above the 10 µF minimum; see [part selection](part-selection.md). Guaranteed minima remain open.
- Calculate rail loads from the selected FPGA configuration, FT2232H, oscillator, flash, and support circuitry. The initial 150/200/30 mA allowances are budgets, not measured current.
- Check the external 5 V supply rating, connector polarity, fuse hold/trip current, Schottky forward loss, reverse-current behavior, and total startup current. Confirm USB VBUS remains a sense input even during fault and suspend states.
- Resolve power-good low-level noise margin and test startup/brownout sequencing.
- Review package land patterns, exposed-pad mappings, and full-board netlist before placement. Configuration pins are `CRESET_B` pin 8 and `CDONE` pin 7; the master-SPI pins are 14–17.

The captured schematic builds and its generated netlist passes targeted pin/rail and USB/FTDI checks. No rail simulation, reviewed board footprints, fabricated board, or physical power-up observation is available yet.
